A field guide for non-musicians

You do not need theory.
You need good constraints.

Canopy keeps notes in one scale, uses proven chord paths, and changes only a few musical dimensions at once. Your job is to choose the feeling.

01A reliable five-minute workflowStart here for every new game area
01Choose a world

Pick a key and scale. Major or Lydian feels bright; Dorian feels ancient; Minor feels shadowed.

02Choose a chord path

Open sky and Homeward are dependable. The Roman numerals are just movable chord positions.

03Compose for me

Generate a motif, then remove notes you dislike. Space usually sounds better than complexity.

04Test danger

Move the Threat control slowly. Listen for the same identity surviving as the energy rises.

05Export both

Use the Tone.js runtime in your game and MIDI if you want to polish the idea in a DAW later.

MIDI or Tone.js?

Use both, for different jobs.

MIDI is a compact list of notes, timing, and velocity. It is excellent for importing melodies and moving sketches between music tools, but it does not define the sound or how a score should react to danger.

Tone.js is the realtime instrument and conductor. It schedules notes precisely, runs effects, and lets your game change layers and parameters while the music is playing.

02Only the words you needNo conservatory required
Key

The note that feels like home. Changing it moves the whole song higher or lower.

Scale

A safe palette of notes. Harmony guard prevents notes outside this palette.

Chord

Several notes heard together. Chords create the emotional landscape beneath a melody.

Motif

A short, memorable musical idea. Repetition makes it recognizable; variation keeps it alive.

Density

How often notes happen. Low density is spacious; high density adds urgency.

Reverb

The impression of a physical space. More reverb feels distant, dreamlike, or vast.
